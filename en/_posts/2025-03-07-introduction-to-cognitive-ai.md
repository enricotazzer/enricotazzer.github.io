Cognitive science is an interdisciplinary field that studies the human mind and its processes, drawing on linguistics, psychology, neuroscience, philosophy, computer science and anthropology. Today, the meeting point of cognitive science and technology is redefining human-machine interaction, and at the heart of this revolution is Cognitive Artificial Intelligence (Cognitive AI).

## What is Cognitive AI?

Cognitive AI is an advanced form of artificial intelligence that doesn't just analyze data statistically or through predefined rules, but imitates human thought processes and learning abilities. This sets it apart from traditional machine learning and deep learning systems: it doesn't just solve specific tasks, it keeps evolving by learning from past experience.

The ultimate goal of Cognitive AI is to build adaptive systems that can recognize patterns, make inferences, reason and interact in an increasingly natural way, getting closer to how the human brain works (although reproducing it faithfully remains a utopian goal).

## How does Cognitive AI work? Architecture and key components

The architecture of a cognitive AI system is designed to replicate different aspects of human thought through a set of core modules:

##### 1. Parsing: understanding the data

Parsing analyzes the input data (text, speech or images) and breaks it down into meaningful units, just as the human brain does when it processes language and visual information.

##### 2. Learning: continuous learning

A cognitive AI system is designed to keep absorbing new information and adapting its knowledge over time. Thanks to advanced learning techniques, the model sharpens its ability to recognize patterns and correlations, improving its performance through experience.

##### 3. Inference: reasoning and deduction

Inference is the ability to draw logical conclusions from what has been learned. This module lets Cognitive AI make decisions, make predictions and build deductions in an increasingly refined way, simulating the human reasoning process.

##### 4. Language Generation: producing language

Language generation lets AI systems compose coherent, context-aware responses by analyzing the tone, meaning and intent of the input they receive. The goal is to make human-machine interaction as natural as possible.

##### 5. Context Awareness: the role of context

Understanding context is essential for giving appropriate and relevant answers. This module analyzes surrounding information and previous interactions, keeping the conversation continuous.

##### 6. Memory: simulating human memory

Memory lets the system emulate human memory (storing and recalling past information): it keeps relevant information from past interactions, avoids repetition and makes conversations more coherent. This is essential for tools like ChatGPT, which have to keep track of the conversation and put each new request in context.

##### The real value of Cognitive AI: constant learning

What really sets cognitive AI apart from traditional models is its ability to keep learning from real experience. The more it interacts with the world, the more it sharpens its reasoning, giving ever clearer, better articulated and more context-aware answers.

## Cognitive AI: reality vs science fiction

While Cognitive AI represents the future of artificial intelligence, it's important to avoid overly optimistic or science-fiction visions. We need to keep our feet on the ground: these are still systems that work and think in 1s and 0s, able to emulate (not simulate, that would be utopian) some phases of human learning, not least because even we don't know exactly how our own brain works.

For example, applications such as self-driving cars are often called "cognitive", but in my (perhaps mistaken) view they are simply systems still based on advanced deep learning models, in the sense that they respond to predefined inputs and stimuli without any real human-like "reasoning". These systems can carry out actions in sophisticated ways, but they have no awareness or autonomous decision-making in the human sense of the term.

True cognitive AI is still a research goal, and a "human computer" is a long way off. Still, current developments are laying the foundations for a future in which artificial intelligence can truly think and learn in ways ever closer to our own.
