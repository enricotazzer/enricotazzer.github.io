Over the last few years, huge advances in technology, especially in hardware, and the explosion of big data have made it possible to use Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools, which need large amounts of data and substantial computing resources to be effective.

Leaving aside the history of the discipline, I'll just say that although these tools have existed for over 30 years, their success and use have exploded only in recent years, particularly in Computer Vision and Natural Language Processing (NLP), where we find applications such as ChatGPT.

I believe that progress in these areas has reached the limit of its potential, so these tools now need to be applied to other fields, where they can bring significant improvements to everyday life. That is why I think researchers' study and effort should flow towards medicine.

## Inside the healthcare sector

This field has always kept its distance from computing and stayed 'old-fashioned', favoring paper records (inherently error-prone) and lacking universal standards for creating and managing clinical documents.

Beyond the administrative side, AI tools would also make care more effective at the clinical level. In diagnosis, for example, they would increase accuracy and get around the problem of misdiagnosis by giving 'a first medical opinion' on the case. This would help doctors limit the number of follow-up tests, which are often useless because they started from a wrong diagnosis.

In this context, preventive medicine can really take off, producing reports on a person's condition and alerting them to suspicious situations that call for an expert opinion. Besides providing a 'personal health assistant', this approach can bring another benefit: fewer people going to hospital, only those who actually need a medical opinion, which reduces doctors' workload.

To conclude, I want to stress the central point of my argument: AI is a tool that supports doctors, and it should never cross our minds that one day doctors will be replaced by machines (that's a fantasy). Although the term Artificial Intelligence is misleading, it isn't intelligent at all: we are still talking about machines that work only with 0s and 1s. They are certainly 'luxury assistants', but they have no empathy, humanity, reasoning, problem solving or ability to interpret unusual situations — all qualities that an experienced doctor has and studied hard to acquire.
